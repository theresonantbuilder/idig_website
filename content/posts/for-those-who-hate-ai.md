I have noticed a palpable tone around the pushback on Generative AI where I find myself in more heated exchanges on LinkedIn than I am accustomed to. But I will continue to lean into defending what I believe AI can become, which is a useful tool to incorporate more human agency over our experiences.

Granted, there are serious issues beyond the apocalyptic undertones of our AI overlords. One being the fact that generative AI is stealing our words, our voice, and our art. No doubt we do need laws in place to enforce the rights of people who have been directly copied by the models. But this notion that training data is thievery in general does not register with me.

The funny thing is the last people I want to defend are leaders of trillion dollar companies as it is the "same as it ever was" as the Talking Heads would say. But let's think about the mechanics of Generative AI.

Even though I am far from an AI expert, I do know a little about the fundamentals of the technology — where words are assembled around predictive patterns to formulate expressions of writing, images, moving images, data, or code.

So, when a musical artist's songs and image are copied, and bad actors steal their identity through AI, this is wrong. When an actor is cloned and their performance is used without their permission, this should be illegal. Full stop.

But the idea that the models are stealing our words falls flat to me. If AI is stealing the corpus of human language to use it for the benefit of others, what is the difference between me sitting in a college classroom while absorbing words from a professor, then taking that knowledge to wrap it up in a book with a copyright?

Yes, there is a fine line of assembling the words of an author and copying their exact prose and style, but if the words are not copied note for note, why would AI be accused of theft if a human wouldn't be?

Am I not downloading training data into my brain as I engage in life and use that data to help myself in my professional and personal life? This is what the models are doing; they are learning, inferring, and sharing. My point is that it is not the fault of AI; it is the fault of people using AI in malicious ways. Bad intent negatively impacts AI just like it negatively impacts every other morsel of human communication.

The point might be brought up that the scale of AI lifting all of human knowledge is an entirely different game. But there are 8 billion people on the planet ingesting training data, whether it is words spoken from a neighbor or reading War and Peace. Yes, AI can distribute this mesh of knowledge immediately to a much wider audience than a human could, but this seems to be a human problem of distributive choice rather than AI scaling on its own accord.

A perfect example of this is the process of scanning every copyrighted book on the planet to teach AI how to think and reason. If War and Peace were effectively copied and shipped. This would be a problem. If Tolstoy's character arcs and tone were copied. This would also be a problem, as it would be if a human did the same in turn.

But is ingesting all knowledge ever generated by humans, then turning this into a reasoning model for others to engage with, a problem? I don't think it is. Knowledge is power, and you want to know what is even more powerful? Agency over knowledge. The more this agency is distributed across the populace, the healthier the outcomes! And I believe Generative AI and authentic AI used in a healthy manner is the path forward to a healthier society.

How do we manifest the healthy building and use of AI? I would say spend more time showing the way rather than hating the way.

If you want AI to be the bogeyman, then at least call out with equal fervor how all of our experiences are being hijacked by silos and sold and used to commodify our attention.

Again, let's set rules of governance to control explicit theft from people using these tools, but I'm just getting more of a sense of people wanting to throw out the baby with the bath water. There is policy, and there are people. Set clear legal boundaries, but not at the expense of human agency.

I've been let down by every iteration of communication technology since I discovered the World Wide Web in 1992, but generative AI feels different to me. It can be a lever that listens if we feed it with good intent, which can broaden our horizons.

As much as AI providers would like to profit off of people through this transformative set of tools, I don't believe we have to give up on the quality of engagement like what happened with the web and social media.

Yes, AI can be manipulative if we are not fully engaged and can even lead us down hazardous roads, but so have people. Or is it the autonomy that scares us when a machine is manipulating or influencing our decisions? We were there well before Gen AI.

Is it that an infinitely smart machine will autonomously manipulate us? This is a much bigger problem than the theft of knowledge. This is what we should fight against. Again, let's not throw out the baby.

I'm not rooting for Anthropic, Meta, Grok, Gemini, or OpenAI. I am rooting for all of us to harness these tools to build a more resonant future.

Yes, actors will be replaced by AI, but I don't think they will ever go away. The physical book has held on quite well, so I believe there will be a heavy demand for live actors capturing our imagination for many years to come. But I don't see how a significant change in the entertainment industry is not upon us.

Unfortunately, this will affect livelihoods that will have to be recalibrated, but I also believe music, sports, writing, and the art of expression have become overly commodified.

People take the knowledge they have accumulated from other sources through their own training data and expect to become rich from it. They absorb this corpus to run better on the field, write a better book, and act a little bit more realistically.

Whatever happened to the love of the game? To write for passion, to play a sport for passion, or to capture an idea in a medium for passion.

I am not saying everyone does not create for passion, but I believe we have witnessed a steady march of the creative process becoming more of a commodity than pure expression.

Were we given the gift of creativity and the drive to create to become rich or to enrich? Is AI competing with us for these expressions, or is it giving us a chance to better enrich ourselves and others with the experiences we create?

Isn't AI just a paintbrush after all? Isn't it just a tool? The hand holds the ego. Not the tool.

I may be naïve in giving the benefit of the doubt to people who use AI with good intent, but I also don't think that since the dawn of humans have we seen such a toolset to help us become the best version of ourselves to then favorably impact people around us. I am an optimist. Guilty as charged.

Although, there is one area I am concerned with, which is the amount of control AI providers will have in this future. When an AI company is looking to raise $2 trillion in an IPO, institutional investors will expect to see healthy returns, which will demand endless growth. Endless growth and quality engagement never have lived in the same universe.

But what is possible when local open source LLM's combined with increasingly powerful local compute happens? When people start to host their own AI solutions that are not controlled by centralized governance. Will these local models be throttled by a collusion between big business and government? Quite possibly, but I am not so sure they can put this genie back in the bottle.

For one, I believe there are too many people building off of this localized foundation, and secondly there is an increasing population of people starting to use these models to benefit their daily lives. The more we use these tools to better our own realities, the more difficult it will be to control them. This is due to personal agency being built into the fabric of the tools. I argue in the end, this will make the tools more difficult to control with a centralized approach.

But if we go into this thinking we're going to get a wizard, we will get the warlock. And if we don't engage our kids around a healthy usage of these tools, which probably should include some form of age gating, we could see problems arise. Just like a gun can be put to good use to protect our families or could be used to harm, AI could be put to use to help ourselves and the health of others, or the reverse will happen. We have to remain vigilant in using these tools purposefully, or else.

As far as all that could go wrong when AI becomes super-intelligent, I don't know what the future holds, but I'm guessing it would probably benefit all of us to be ready rather than sticking our heads in the sand.

Let's get to work and build amazing things for people of all walks of life to harness. To build to lift ourselves and others into a more purposeful societal connection. Yes, there are threats. Yes, there are problems with the fundamentals of the technology. But hating on the tech is not a solution. It is a distraction on what this technology could become if we use it purposefully.
